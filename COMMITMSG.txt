Astra: "Milfuel is manufactured at Navy/Syndicate reprocessing plants."

Half of that was already true and the other half was a hole in the fiction
this file had already argued for. The Syndicate's whole racket IS the
waste -- they ban radioactive cargo in the systems they hold and dump
their own in everyone else's back garden -- so a hold is the one other
place with both the feedstock and nobody to answer to. And it could not
happen by accident: no naval garrison ever spawns in a hold (the
`sys.pirateHeld` gate in buildPatrols) and a passing cutter licenses
nothing, so before this the single most lawless place in the galaxy was
the one place you could not buy military fuel.

`licensor(sys)` answers navy, syndicate or nobody, and reads a flag
generate.js has already set -- NO RNG DRAW, the same discipline the rest
of this pass keeps, which is what makes the licence additive to every
pre-existing seed.

A syndicate plant keeps ten points less back than a naval one. The navy's
share is a contract; the Syndicate's is a cut, and a cut is smaller than a
requisition, because they are selling -- which is the point of holding the
plant. So a hold is the better place to buy it and the worst place to be
caught leaving with it.

WORTH A LOOK BEFORE THIS IS SETTLED: it roughly triples how much of the
galaxy breeds the stuff. The doc's figure was 16 of 150 systems, all with
a naval garrison; holds are about 18% of the galaxy and every one of them
has a plant, so it lands near a third of systems rather than a ninth. The
margin argument still holds -- the navy contract is what stops it being a
risk-free 3.5x run -- but availability is a separate lever and this pulled
it. economy.test.js prints the count every run so the number is visible
rather than inferred.

AND THE SUITE IS GREEN. economy.test.js has failed on every tagged build
since v0.1.0-alpha5 on 2026-09-07 -- "no world manufactures above its
development level, 17 violations" -- which short-circuits `npm test`, so
the four suites after it never ran, the package step never ran, and the
release job never ran. Every release this repo has made was made by hand
past a gate that was red the whole time.

The violations had a shape nobody had looked at: all eighteen were
milfuel, at development 0.124 to 0.508. One commodity, never a spread.
Licensed output is not manufacture -- a licence is a thing you are given,
not a thing you develop into -- so the development rule now excludes it,
and a STRICTER rule takes its place: military fuel is only ever bred at a
reprocessing plant, only in a system that licenses it, and only burned
where naval hulls are serviced. Six new checks; the Syndicate one fails on
the old code.
